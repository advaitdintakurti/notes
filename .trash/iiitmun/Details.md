
# KNOW YOUR DELEGATION – COUNTRY PROFILE: GERMANY

## General

- **Conventional Long Form Country Name:** Federal Republic of Germany
- **Government Type:** Federal parliamentary republic
- **Chief of State:** President Frank‑Walter Steinmeier
- **Head of Government:** Chancellor Olaf Scholz
- **Language(s):**
    - **German** (official language)
    - Recognized regional languages: Sorbian, Frisian, and others in specific regions
- **Major Religions:**
    - **Christianity:** Approximately 27% Roman Catholic and 26% Protestant
    - **Islam:** Around 5%
    - **Non-religious/Other:** Roughly 35–40% of the population (including atheists, agnostics, and smaller religious groups)  
        _(Note: Percentages are approximate and vary by source.)_

## History

- **Independence & Unification:**
    - **19th Century:** Modern Germany emerged as a unified nation-state in 1871 under Prussian leadership, forming the German Empire.
    - **Post‑World War II:** Following defeat in WWII, Germany was divided into West Germany (Federal Republic of Germany, established in 1949) and East Germany (German Democratic Republic).
    - **Reunification:** The modern, unified Federal Republic of Germany was re-established in 1990 following the fall of the Berlin Wall and the collapse of East Germany.
- **Colonial History & Occupation:**
    - **Colonial Era:** Between the 1880s and World War I, Germany controlled colonies in Africa (e.g., German South‑West Africa [now Namibia], Togo, Cameroon, German East Africa [parts of modern Tanzania, Rwanda, and Burundi]) as well as several Pacific islands.
    - **Post‑WWI:** These colonies were lost following defeat in World War I and subsequent Treaty of Versailles settlements.
    - **Occupation:** After WWII, Germany was occupied by Allied forces (1945–1949) until the establishment of West Germany.

- **Size:** Approximately 357,022 km² (137,988 sq. miles)

    - **North:** Denmark

- **Major Ports:**
    - **Hamburg:** Germany’s largest port and a major European logistics hub

## Standard of Living

- **Annual Income:**
    - GDP per capita is roughly USD 50,000 (nominal figure; varies by year)
- **Literacy Rate:** Approximately 99% (based on adult literacy rates from recent estimates)
- **Birth Rate:**
    - Around 9–10 births per 1,000 people per year
    - Average fertility rate is approximately 1.5 children per woman
- **Death Rate:** Approximately 11 per 1,000 people per year
- **Infant Mortality Rate:** Roughly 3 deaths per 1,000 live births
- **Unemployment Rate:** Typically in the range of 3–5% in recent years
- **Major Ethnic/Cultural Issues:**
    - Predominantly ethnic Germans with significant minority communities (e.g., Turks, Eastern Europeans)
    - Ongoing debates and policy challenges related to immigration, integration, and the rise of far‑right extremism
- **Current Refugee/IDP Concerns:**
    - Germany is a key destination for refugees (notably from Syria, Afghanistan, and more recently Ukraine)
    - Integration and social cohesion remain important political and social challenges

## Politics

- **Political Allies/Blocs:**
    - Core member of the European Union (EU)
    - Founding and active member of NATO
    - Member of the G7 and close strategic ties with the United States
- **Conflicts: Past and Present:**
    - **Past:** Involved in both World Wars, Cold War divisions, and the challenges of reunification
    - **Present:** No active military conflicts; internal political debates persist on issues such as immigration, economic disparities, and national identity
- **Political Participation:**
    - Citizens enjoy broad democratic rights; voting age is 18
    - Freedom of speech and press is guaranteed, with legal limitations to prevent hate speech and extremist incitement
- **National Elections & Political Parties:**
    - Regular national elections for the Bundestag
    - A vibrant multi‑party system including major parties such as the CDU/CSU, SPD, Greens, FDP, Die Linke, and AfD, among others

## Economy

- **GDP and Growth Rate:**
    - Nominal GDP is around USD 4 trillion
    - Growth rates typically range between 1.5–2% in a mature, developed economy
- **Major Trade Partners:**
    - EU member states (e.g., France, Netherlands, Italy)
    - The United States, China, and the United Kingdom
- **Major Exports (and approximate total export value):**
    - Automobiles, machinery, chemical products, electrical equipment, and pharmaceuticals
    - Exports valued at roughly USD 1.5 trillion (figures vary by year)
- **Major Imports (and approximate total import value):**
    - Machinery, chemicals, raw materials, oil, natural gas, and food products
    - Imports valued at around USD 1.3 trillion
- **Overseas Development Aid (ODA):**
    - **ODA Given:** One of the world’s top donors; in recent years, Germany has provided in the vicinity of €25 billion (approximately USD 27 billion)
    - **ODA Received:** Negligible, as Germany is a major donor rather than a recipient
- **Agricultural Products:** Cereals (wheat, barley), potatoes, sugar beets, dairy products, pork
- **Industries:**
    - Automotive manufacturing, machinery, chemical production, electrical and electronic equipment, pharmaceuticals, and renewable energy technology
- **Natural Resources:**
    - Limited; includes lignite, coal, natural gas (mostly imported), salt, and timber
- **Energy Sources (approximate shares as per recent data):**
    - Renewables: ~46% of electricity generation
    - Coal: ~20%
    - Natural Gas: ~15%
    - Oil: ~13%
    - Nuclear Energy: ~6% (Germany is in the process of phasing out nuclear power)

## Military

- **Military Expenditures:**
    - Roughly 1.3–1.5% of GDP
- **Major Weapons, Arsenal, Nuclear Capability:**
    - The Bundeswehr is equipped with advanced systems such as the Leopard 2 main battle tank, Eurofighter Typhoon jets, and modern naval vessels
    - Germany does not possess its own nuclear weapons; it participates in NATO’s nuclear sharing arrangements
- **Regional Threats:**
    - No immediate military threat from neighboring countries; however, geopolitical concerns persist (e.g., tensions with Russia, particularly given its nuclear arsenal, and broader regional security issues)

---

## UN Relations

- **Date Admitted to the UN:**
    - West Germany was admitted on 18 September 1973; unified Germany maintained membership following reunification in 1990
- **UN Dues Payment Status:**
    - Germany is a major contributor and consistently meets its financial obligations to the UN
- **Universal Declaration of Human Rights:**
    - Yes, Germany is a signatory and upholds its principles
- **UN Intervention in Conflicts:**
    - The UN has not directly intervened in internal conflicts within Germany
    - However, post‑World War II reconstruction and various UN peacekeeping efforts have been part of the broader international framework in which Germany participates
